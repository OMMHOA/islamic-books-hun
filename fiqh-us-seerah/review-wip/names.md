# Names — Hungarian forms

**How to use this file:** each line reads `current spelling → Hungarian form (how many times)`.
Where you disagree, overwrite the **bold** form, or just tell me in chat. Whatever you leave
alone counts as accepted. Then I apply it to the whole book (with the Hungarian suffixes, e.g.
Quraisht → Kurajsot) and re-sort the glossary.

Generated by `review-wip/names_build.py`. 1229 different names/terms occur in the
book; only the ones worth a look are listed. **The rest (mostly names that occur once or twice)
simply get the rule**, and you will see them in the ebook.

## The rule (decided 2026-09-28)

- long vowels ā ī ū → á í ú · dotted letters lose the dot: ḥ→h, ṭ→t, ḍ→d, ẓ→z, ṣ→sz
- sh→s, s→sz, th→sz, dh→z, j→dzs, y→j, w→v, q→k, aw→au, ay→aj
- **kh and gh stay** everywhere (Khálid, Ghatafán)
- the ʿayn/hamza apostrophe is dropped (Sa'd → Szad); final -ah → -a (Ḥamzah → Hamza)
- the article stays **al-**, not assimilated (al-Zubajr, al-Tirmizí); lowercase except at a sentence start
- Abū → Abu, Banū → Banú; ibn, bint, Umm unchanged
- prophets get their **Hungarian biblical names** (Ábrahám, Mózes); other people with the same name follow
  the rule (Ibn Iszhák, Abu Múszá, Abu Dávúd)
- already done: Mohamed (the Prophet), Muhammad (everyone else), Mekka, Medina, Jézus
- glossary headwords follow the same forms, and the glossary is re-sorted afterwards

## 1. Questions for you (9)

The rule can't settle these. My pick is after the arrow; change it if you disagree.

**1. Khosrau** (24×, the Persian king): the rule gives *Khoszrau*; Hungarian history books write *Huszrau* (II. Huszrau). One place has the Arabic title instead: „Kisra városát”.  
→ **Huszrau**

**2. Negus** (13× capitalised, 3× already *négus*): Hungarian spells the Abyssinian king's title lowercase: *a négus*.  
→ **négus**

**3. Surah names** (Sūrat al-Tawbah, Sūrat ul-Lahab, Sūrah Maryam, Sūrah Hūd, Sūrah Fuṣṣilat, Sūrah Barā'ah…): the rule alone would give *Szúrat al-Tauba*. Hungarian word order, without the Arabic article, reads better.  
→ **a Tauba szúra, a Lahab szúra, a Marjam szúra, a Húd szúra**

**4. Month names** (51×: Ramaḍān, Shawwāl, Dhul-Ḥijjah…): Hungarian writes month names lowercase (like *január*): *ramadán*, *savvál*, *zul-hiddzsa*.  
→ **lowercase**

**5. Banū Isrā'īl** (1×; the book already says *Izrael fiai* 15×): the rule gives *Banú Iszráíl*.  
→ **Banú Iszráíl**

**6. Book titles** (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, Fatḥ al-Bārī, Al-Bidāyah, Sunan, Taqrīb…): follow the rule too (*Szahíh al-Bukhárí*, *Fath al-Bárí*, *al-Bidája*), or keep the transliteration as a bibliographic reference?  
→ **the rule**

**7. The author's name** (title page and signature: *Muhammad Al-Ghazali*): the rule gives *Muhammad al-Ghazálí*; the English edition's cover has *al-Ghazali*.  
→ **Muhammad al-Ghazáli**

**8. Arabic sentences** (adhān and shahāda, e.g. *Ashhadu an lā ilāha illa-llāh*): follow the rule too (*Ashadu an lá iláha illa-lláh*), so a Hungarian reader pronounces them right?  
→ **yes, the rule**

**9. Transliteration table** (back matter, „# Átírási táblázat”): explains the ā/ḥ/ṣ letters, which the Hungarian text no longer uses. Replace it with a short note on how names are written (the rule above)?  
→ **replace with a note**
